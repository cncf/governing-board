Additionally, Ms. Lee noted that a draft of the new Governing Board Operations Manual had been circulated to GB members via email. The manual consolidates all board policies and procedures into a single place, covering topics such as quorum, voting, and conflicts of interest. Members were asked to review the draft and provide comments by September 10, after which a proposed final version incorporating comments will be voted on via email.

**Budget Update**  
Mr. Bryce reviewed the 2026 midyear reforecast, noting that the budget is tracking ahead of plan, driven by strong membership revenue and a financially successful KubeCon \+ CloudNativeCon EU. He noted that expenses were slightly increased across most areas, with a specific allocation in Developer Collaboration to fund the GPU testing program, which is directly offset by GPU program revenue. Discussion ensued.

**Membership Updates**  
Highlighting new Platinum members Adyen, Broadcom, and CoreWeave, Mr. Bryce noted that their varied backgrounds prove the CNCF's relevance across many different industries. He also welcomed several new Silver members, emphasizing the value of end user participation, and noted that another Platinum member is expected by year-end.

**Event Updates**  
Ms. Heric provided an overview of recent foundation events, noting overall strong attendance and sponsorship support. She highlighted that KubeCon \+ CloudNativeCon Amsterdam was the largest to date with over 13,400 attendees, and the North American Observability Summit doubled its attendance from 2025\. While sponsorships in Europe and North America remain steady, the team is exploring strategies to increase sponsorship numbers in India and Japan.

Looking ahead, Ms. Heric reported strong registration and sponsorship pacing for upcoming events, including KubeCon \+ CloudNativeCon Japan this week, KubeCon \+ CloudNativeCon \+ OpenInfra Summit \+ PyTorch Conference China 2026 in September, and KubeCon \+ CloudNativeCon North America in Salt Lake City, which has nearly reached its sponsorship goal. She also outlined a robust schedule of regional and virtual events for the fall.

Finally, Ms. Heric announced the launch of the 2027 sponsorship prospectus for KubeCon \+ CloudNativeCon Europe and North America. Due to high demand, the Diamond-level sponsorship lottery will return, with priority given to Platinum and Gold members.

**Education Updates**  
Mr. Aniszczyk provided updates regarding the continued growth of the Kubestronaut program, noting that there are currently over 460 Golden Kubestonauts.

He also reviewed 2026-Q2 CNCF Education highlights, including that the recertification process has been implemented, Certified Kubernetes Network Engineer (CKNE) will tentatively launch at KubeCon \+ CloudNativeCon NA, and the creation of CNPA and CNPE companion courses has started.

Mr. Aniszczyk also noted that the free Intro to Kubernetes course continues to grow, with a total of more than 440,000 registrants since it launched. 

**Marketing Updates**  
Ms. Royle-Jones reported that the first half of the year focused on making marketing an integrated growth function aligned closely with events and business development. She highlighted that content, including case studies, blogs, and video storytelling, remains the strongest tool for demonstrating momentum and real-world project adoption. Additionally, she noted that expanding into third-party industry events is helping the foundation reach new audiences, particularly AI infrastructure leaders, researchers, and prospective members.

Looking ahead, Ms. Royle-Jones stated her main priority is to build a strong AI narrative around cloud native projects and establish marketing as an amplification engine for the foundation. 

**Project Updates**  
Mr. Aniszczyk noted that a barometer for any foundation to have a long life is to continue to attract high quality projects. He reviewed 2026 project highlights, including the total count of current active projects and recent level changes. 

**Joint Meeting of the Governing Board, Technical Oversight Committee (TOC), and End User Technical Advisory Board (TAB)**  
*The CNCF TOC and TAB joined the meeting at 2:45 PM JST for a joint session of the Governing Board, TOC, and End User TAB*

**TOC Chair Update**  
Ms. Angell reviewed TOC committed activities, including sandbox applications and moving level reviews. She noted that several projects have graduated this year, including Kyverno, OpenTelemetry, Buildpacks, and Kubeflow.

Mr. Wang highlighted the TOC's focus on long-term project health and sustainability, noting that many projects meet moving levels criteria but lack the structures to sustain themselves or have governance documents that reflect aspirations rather than operational reality. To solve this, the TOC is providing increased guidance and proactive coaching before projects transition between maturity levels. Additionally, the TOC is deploying automated checks to shift their focus from manual checklists to high-level evaluation, while actively establishing standardized best practices to help projects manage AI-generated contributions.

A board member asked about succession planning for end-of-life projects. Discussion ensued.

Ms. Angell noted that the TAG restructure is working well, enabling TAGs to move faster. She reviewed enhancement activities, including continuing automation for due diligence steps, better levels guidance for specification projects, and working with LFX Insights and the CNCF  
Projects team for early warning signals for project health. She noted that the TOC is also reviewing adoption patterns for AI-assisted development across CNCF projects.

**End User Program Update**  
Ms. Lee highlighted new end user members since the March 2026 board meeting. 

**TAB Chair Updates**  
Mr. Rocha reviewed recent updates from the End User TAB. Key topics included discussions from public meetings, the status of various reference architectures, the development of the new end user website, preparations for KubeCon \+ CloudNativeCon NA, and the ongoing work on the end user group governance draft.

**Kubernetes AI Conformance Program Update**  
Mr. Bongiovanni briefed the board on the Kubernetes AI Conformance Program. He noted that the initiative has matured from a temporary Working Group to a permanent SIG Architecture subproject, and the ecosystem has quickly expanded to over 31 certified AI platforms across various clouds and providers. Mr. Bongiovanni mentioned that the future roadmap explores advanced inference primitives across routing, caching, and scheduling to ensure open standards.

**NeoNephos Update**  
Mr. Vasilev provided an update on NeoNephos, Europe's neutral open source foundation for cloud-to-edge interoperability. He emphasized that NeoNephos operates as a partner rather than a competitor to CNCF, actively integrating CNCF projects like Kubernetes, containerd, and Helm to address European digital sovereignty needs. He outlined specific areas for future collaboration, proposing a recurring quarterly sync between the NeoNephos TAC and the CNCF TOC and establishing a joint conformance working group to build a European cloud assessment framework. Discussion ensued.

**Breakout Discussions**  
The GB Chairperson Mr. Blecker outlined 3 breakout discussion topics and summarized prior GB discussion about these topics: Cloud Native AI Standardization, AI Cost Management & Observability, and Speed of Agent vs. Human Systems.The board divided into four concurrent breakout groups—comprising three in-person tables and one virtual group—before reconvening to share key takeaways.

Regarding Cloud Native AI Standardization, discussions focused on technical and operational friction across training, inference, and emerging agentic infrastructure. Participants highlighted Kubernetes’ historical strength in incubating external innovations—such as Kueue and agent sandboxing—before merging them upstream.

On AI Cost Management & Observability, members addressed the increasing cost growth associated with observing AI workloads and serving infrastructure. Unmonitored tool usage ("shadow IT") across both enterprise accounts and open-source contributor programs was noted as a key barrier to clear cost attribution. While OpenTelemetry has been broadly adopted as a standard data format, tooling to digest high telemetry volumes at scale remains fragmented.

Addressing the Speed of Agent vs. Human Systems, discussions emphasized that relying on "humans in the loop" for final reviews is becoming a critical scaling bottleneck as agent output increases. Attendees examined identity and trust hurdles, noting how defensive system blocking often leads agents to mask their identities, and noted emerging protocols like x402 micropayments as potential alignment mechanisms. Finally, members highlighted opportunities to deploy agents to resolve agent-created complexity—such as onboarding agents that surface historical repository context—and noted potential collaboration with organizations like the Agentic AI Foundation.

The board meeting concluded at 4:34 PM JST.
